# Remaining height consumers observed in current sources

This follow-up completes the controlled wandering/fleeing/cover scenarios. It does not complete the plan's broader actor integration. These source observations identify concrete next checks; they are not claims that a runtime reproduction has already passed.

- `src/wildlife.cpp`, `meleeTarget`: the player-height gate is an upper bound against `groundHeight`; candidate distance/facing and `clearLine` use XZ and ground-height rays. A player under a retained roof can satisfy these bounds against a surface animal. Reproduce surface/underground melee selection and predator attacks with the same XZ positions, then use actual vertical overlap and geometry-aware sight lines.
- `src/wildlife.cpp`, `canMount`/`riderPose`/`dismount`: mounting compares the player with the heightfield surface, and riding/dismounting derive Y directly from that surface. `src/dx11_scene.cpp`, `animals`, likewise roots living animal meshes at `terrain::height`. Inspect animal support, movement, mounting and recovery on excavated floors and placed blocks, including roofs over underground passages. Merely changing one query cannot prove coherent rendering/collision/interaction.
- `src/game.cpp`, the pickup loop after wildlife/bird updates: available original weapon pickups are collected when XZ distance is under 24 and the player is on foot. There is no corresponding vertical overlap check there. Reproduce a pickup above a retained roof with an underground player, and compare the collection rule to the pickup's rendered/support height.
- Remaining armed attack strafing in `src/ai.cpp` still chooses tactical points through `solid(destination,12)`. Its movement is height-aware, but tactical destination selection needs the same kind of actual-floor review as the wandering/cover follow-up. Vehicle road pursuit and other shared 2D consumers also remain open.

Source-derived falling fragments are cleared by `jolt_world::refreshScenery` during a layer reconstruction, so this inspection does not establish an immediate fragment-collision leak into normal mode. Their mode ownership/lifetime and actor recovery still need broader review; do not assume that a source-name search alone proves a behavioral failure.

Any further tests should preserve the root save, run in the separate runtime, use actual Jolt collision and mode transitions, and retain clear limits on fixture scope. Continuous progression, reconstruction rollback/interrupted-process recovery, face/collider merging and dense-world performance remain separate requirements.
